Editor’s note: All four reviewers agreed Trump wrote this and that the pardon and the Tijerina-vs-Cuellar race are real. They split on the brother’s job and charges, and on how soon Cuellar said he would stay a Democrat. Primary sources show Martin Cuellar Jr. is a constable indicted in a separate food-program case, and that Cuellar only committed to remaining a Democrat about three months after the pardon, so those two central lines are false rather than minor slips. That is why the post is misleading, not mostly truthful.

Claims checked

  1. True
    “Trump was headed to Texas and Tano Tijerina is running against Democrat Henry Cuellar.”
    A GOP event listing shows a Trump rally in San Antonio on Oct. 7, 2026. Tijerina, the Republican Webb County judge, is the GOP nominee against incumbent Democrat Henry Cuellar in Texas’s 28th District. [1][2]
  2. Mostly true
    “Trump pardoned Henry Cuellar and his wife at the request of their daughters.”
    Trump granted full pardons to Rep. Henry Cuellar and Imelda Cuellar on Dec. 3, 2025, before trial. He publicly tied the pardons to a plea from their daughters; that motive is his account, not something established by the pardon warrant itself. The quoted dialogue is not independently verified as verbatim. [3][4][5]
  3. Misleading
    “They would have gone to jail on federal fraud and conspiracy charges for over 20 years.”
    In May 2024 they were indicted on bribery, money-laundering conspiracy, and unlawful foreign-influence charges that carried statutory maximums well above 20 years. They had not been convicted or sentenced, so a 20-plus-year term is a possible maximum, not a sentence they would have served. [6][7]
  4. False
    “Henry Cuellar’s brother, a sheriff, was indicted a short time later on the same charges.”
    Martin Cuellar Jr. is a Webb County constable, not the sheriff. He was indicted in May 2026 on separate federal fraud, theft, and money-laundering charges tied to a COVID-era food program, not the foreign-bribery case against Henry and Imelda. [8][9]
  5. False
    “Cuellar announced only a few days after the pardon that he would remain a Democrat.”
    The pardon was Dec. 3, 2025. The next day Cuellar said he was still considering a party switch. He announced in early March 2026, about three months later, that he would stay a Democrat and seek reelection. [4][10][11]
  6. Unverifiable
    “Trump made no deal in exchange for the pardon.”
    The published pardon is a full unconditional pardon. No public evidence of a formal quid pro quo has surfaced, but Trump’s claim about his private intent cannot be independently verified. [3][5]
  7. Opinion
    “Cuellar’s wife wanted him to switch parties, and Democrats persecuted him because they did not like him.”
    The wife’s preference is presented as hearsay (“as I heard”) and is not publicly confirmed. Calling the Biden-era Justice Department case political persecution is a characterization, not a checkable fact.
  8. Mostly true
    “Tano Tijerina is a legendary baseball star and respected county judge who loves Laredo and South Texas.”
    Tijerina pitched in the minor leagues and has been Webb County judge since 2014. “Legendary Baseball Star” is promotional exaggeration; “Loredo” is a misspelling of Laredo. [2][12]
  9. Opinion
    “Henry Cuellar is a disloyal loser who should now be serving a long prison sentence with his wife.”
    Insults and a view of the punishment he deserves are opinions. They were pardoned before any conviction or sentence.
