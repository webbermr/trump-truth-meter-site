Claims checked

  1. False
    “Chicago is suffering an unprecedented and particularly violent crime wave.”
    Chicago Police Department figures show 321 homicides through September 30, 2026, down 3% from 332 a year earlier and about 29% below the same point in 2024. 2025 ended with the fewest homicides in roughly 60 years. Chicago’s homicide rate remains high compared with many other U.S. cities, but that is not an unprecedented wave. [1][2][3]
  2. False
    “The crime wave is getting worse by the day.”
    Year-to-date shootings were down about 4% and overall violent crime about 8% through September. September had 31 murders, down 31% from September 2025 and the fewest for that month since 2010. Early 2026 ran slightly above 2025 before summer and fall reversed it. A few property offenses, such as nonresidential burglary, have risen, but violent crime is not escalating daily. [1][2][3]
  3. Opinion
    “The federal government can extinguish Chicago’s violence quickly and completely.”
    This is a boast and a prediction about a hypothetical intervention, not a checkable fact.
  4. Misleading
    “The mayor of Chicago or the governor of Illinois must ask before the federal government can help.”
    Trump has treated a local request as his preferred trigger, and some National Guard authorities depend on state consent. It is not a general bar. Federal prosecutors and agents already operate in Chicago without a mayoral or gubernatorial invitation, and the administration has tried to deploy Guard troops over local objections. Courts, including the Supreme Court in December 2025, have restricted that military deployment. [4][5][6]
  5. True
    “The mayor and governor are not asking for his help.”
    Mayor Brandon Johnson and Governor JB Pritzker have repeatedly refused Trump’s offers of a federal crime crackdown and have opposed and litigated deployments of federal agents and out-of-state National Guard troops. [6][7]
