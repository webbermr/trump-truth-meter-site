Claims checked

  1. False
    “Europe has just agreed to release a massive amount of its diesel stocks.”
    On Oct. 2, EU officials and G7 partners were discussing a French proposal—not announcing a concluded agreement. Reporting described talks, technical assessment, and a possible later G7 call. A French spokesperson treated reports of the proposal as rumors. The Commission said the Energy Union Task Force examined coordination measures, not that a release had been agreed. [1][2][3][4][5]
  2. False
    “The diesel-release process will begin immediately.”
    No government or the European Commission announced that a new diesel release had been approved or had started. Any further release was still under discussion, and European officials wanted it tied to a U.S. pledge not to ban diesel exports. The Commission referred to an earlier stock release launched in March, not a new process beginning that day. [1][2][3][5]
  3. Mostly true
    “Europe’s diesel reserves are heavily stocked.”
    EU emergency diesel and gasoil stocks are large—on the order of roughly 280–315 million barrels in recent figures, concentrated in France and Germany—so “heavily stocked” is a fair description. That does not make the claimed agreement or immediate release true. [6]
