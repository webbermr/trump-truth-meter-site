Claims checked

  1. True
    “A September 2026 Fox News poll found about 4% of registered voters named Iran or the war as the country’s most important issue.”
    Fox’s September 11–14, 2026 national poll of registered voters recorded 4% for the open-ended top issue “Iran / War,” well behind the economy and other domestic issues. [1][2]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Sep 27).
  2. Misleading
    “The 4% figure shows Americans support the Iran action, or that the poll is evidence the action was right rather than wrong.”
    Issue salience is not approval. In the same Fox poll, 60% said the U.S. military action against Iran was the wrong decision and 37% said it was right; 71% said the administration lacked a clear plan to end the war. The video’s claim that the figure would otherwise be about 98% is speculation. [1][2][3]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Sep 27).
  3. False
    “Iran was days away from being able to build a nuclear weapon before Trump acted.”
    IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi said there was no evidence of a structured nuclear-weapons program and rejected the idea that Iran was days or weeks from a bomb. Near-weapons-grade uranium is not a completed weapon; public expert assessments did not support a days-long timeline. [4][5][6]
  4. False
    “Trump eliminated Iran’s nuclear threat.”
    The IAEA’s September 2026 report said it could not determine the size or location of Iran’s enriched-uranium stockpile or verify that enrichment had stopped, and called the lack of access a proliferation concern. U.S. polling in the same period still treated the Iran conflict as ongoing. [4][1]
  5. Misleading
    “Iranian authorities killed tens of thousands of their own people with guns.”
    Security forces did kill protesters with live fire in the 2025–26 crackdown, and some estimates, including figures cited by Amnesty, run into the thousands and possibly as high as about 20,000. A settled toll of “tens of thousands” killed with guns has not been established. [7][8]
  6. Misleading
    “Every president before Trump merely left Iran’s nuclear problem to a successor and did not act.”
    Presidents before Trump did leave a difficult problem unresolved, but Obama’s administration negotiated the 2015 nuclear agreement, which the IAEA verified Iran initially implemented. That was not simple inaction, even if later abandoned. [9]
  7. Opinion
    “If Trump had not acted, Americans would now be living in fear of an Iranian nuclear attack, and roughly 98% would name the war their top issue.”
    These are counterfactuals and rhetorical praise, not measured results.
