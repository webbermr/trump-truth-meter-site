Claims checked

  1. True
    “The House passed the Sunshine Protection Act by a bipartisan vote of 308 to 117.”
    On July 14, 2026, the House passed H.R. 139, the Sunshine Protection Act, 308–117, with majorities of both parties voting yes. [1][2]
    🔁 Checked 2 times before.
  2. False
    “The bill the House approved is called “The No More Ridiculous Changing of Your Clocks Act.””
    That is Trump’s nickname. The measure the House passed is titled the Sunshine Protection Act. [1][2]
  3. Misleading
    “The Sunshine Protection Act would end twice-yearly clock changes by making current daylight saving time permanent, giving Arkansas an extra hour of daylight.”
    The bill would make daylight saving time the permanent standard, ending the clock switch unless a state opts to stay on standard time. It shifts an hour of winter daylight from morning to evening; it does not create more daylight. [1][3]
    🔁 Checked 2 times before.
  4. False
    “Trump won Arkansas three times, by almost 40 points.”
    He carried Arkansas in 2016, 2020, and 2024, but the margins were about 27, 28, and 30.6 points (64.2% to 33.6% in 2024). None was close to 40. [4][5]
    🔁 Checked once before.
  5. Misleading
    “Tom Cotton has held the bill in the Senate for months, and only out of ego.”
    The Senate received the bill on July 15, 2026, and it has stalled, with Cotton a leading opponent. He has publicly argued that permanent daylight saving time would mean dark winter mornings for schoolchildren and commuters, and he is not the only obstacle: other senators oppose it and leadership has doubted a 60-vote path. [2][6][7][8]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Oct 2).
  6. False
    “Year-round daylight saving time was ended because lawmakers used it as a negotiating chip, even though everybody loved it.”
    Congress imposed year-round daylight saving time in January 1974 and repealed the winter portion that October after public support collapsed over children going to school in the dark. It was not swapped for other legislation. [9][10]
  7. Misleading
    “Daylight saving time, meaning permanent afternoon daylight, polls 65–35.”
    A YouGov poll found about 65% want to stop changing clocks, which is not the same claim. When people choose a permanent clock, AP-NORC (2025) found 56% prefer daylight saving time and 42% standard time; a 2026 YouGov poll found 43% preferred permanent daylight saving time when ending the switch was posed as a choice, and another 2026 reading found 51% support for permanent daylight saving time with substantial opposition or uncertainty. [11][12][13]
    🔁 Rated false 2 times before, since Oct 2.
  8. False
    “Not wanting to change clocks twice a year is a 100% issue.”
    Opposition to the twice-yearly switch is a majority, not unanimous. YouGov has found roughly 64–65% want it ended and about 15–16% do not; AP-NORC found 12% still favor the current system. [11][12][13]
    🔁 Rated false 2 times before, since Oct 2.
  9. Mostly true
    “More evening light has been proven to stop crime.”
    Peer-reviewed work, including Doleac and Sanders, finds that the shift to later sunset cuts some evening crimes, especially robbery. “Proven” and “stopping crime” overstate a narrower finding, and it is separate from any claim about Trump’s own crime record. [14][15]
  10. Misleading
    “Permanent daylight saving time is loved by farmers.”
    Golf interests support the bill, but farm groups have historically objected to daylight saving time because it pushes winter sunrise later. The American Farm Bureau has not taken an official position favoring permanent daylight saving time, and Cotton cites farm concerns about dark mornings. [6][16][17]
  11. True
    “The attached PGA Tour letter’s NGCOA figures — 23 million extra rounds, about $1 billion a year, and roughly $250,000 per facility — match the association’s estimates.”
    Those figures appear in National Golf Course Owners Association testimony to Congress. They are industry projections, not measured results. The letter’s account of Trump attending the Presidents Cup at Medinah also matches contemporaneous reporting. [18][19]
