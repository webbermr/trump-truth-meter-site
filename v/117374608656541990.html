Claims checked

  1. False
    “U.S. forces and their peers have destroyed Iran's navy.”
    2025 strikes hit some naval and coastal targets, but Iran’s navy, including the IRGC’s fast-attack and missile boats, was not destroyed and remained a functioning force afterward. [1][2]
    • Also checked by professional fact-checkers:
    • PolitiFact: True on “In 1988, in response to Iran’s attack on the U.S.S. Samuel Roberts, President Ronald Reagan "destroyed half of Iran’s Navy."”
  2. Misleading
    “U.S. forces and their peers have destroyed Iran's air force.”
    Strikes damaged air bases and aircraft and, for a period, established air superiority over Iran, but open assessments did not find the air force eliminated as a service. [1][3]
  3. Mostly true
    “U.S. forces and their peers destroyed Iran's air defense systems.”
    Israeli and U.S. strikes in 2024–2025 substantially degraded Iran’s integrated air defenses, including Russian-supplied systems, though “destroyed” overstates a total, permanent wipeout. [1][3]
  4. Misleading
    “U.S. forces and their peers destroyed Iran's military leadership.”
    Strikes killed a number of senior IRGC and military commanders, but Iran’s political and military leadership was not destroyed; the regime and command structure continued. [1][4]
  5. False
    “U.S. forces and their peers destroyed Iran's military-industrial capacity and entire defense base.”
    Strikes hit military and industrial sites and set programs back, but Iran retained missile production, conventional forces, and a defense establishment. Calling the entire defense base destroyed is not supported. [1][2][5]
  6. Misleading
    “Iran's conventional shield has been crippled and operations are ensuring Iran never gets a nuclear weapon.”
    Conventional defenses and nuclear sites were heavily damaged and the program was set back, but U.S. and outside assessments described a delay of months to a few years, not verified permanent denial of a nuclear weapon or a fully crippled conventional shield. [5][6][7]
  7. Opinion
    “U.S. media makes Iranian state media look reasonable, is driven by political retribution and “Trump Derangement Syndrome,” and hates the President more than the country.”
    This is rhetoric and a value judgment about journalists’ motives, not a checkable factual claim.
