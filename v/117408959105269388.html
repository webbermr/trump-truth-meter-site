Claims checked

    “Mike Rogers is the Republican candidate for U.S. Senate from Michigan and debated his Democratic opponent the night of this post.”
    Rogers, the Republican nominee, and Democrat Abdul El-Sayed held their first Senate debate on WOOD-TV in Grand Rapids on Thursday, Oct. 8, 2026. The post went up shortly after midnight UTC on Oct. 9. [1][2]

    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 8).
  3. False
    “Abdul El-Sayed is a jihadist.”
    He is an American physician and former Detroit and Wayne County health director. Fact-checks find no evidence he is a jihadist, pledged support to the Muslim Brotherhood, or said the U.S. deserved 9/11. [5][6][7]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 6).
  4. False
    “El-Sayed hates the United States, hates Black people in Detroit, and wants the U.S. to fail.”
    No evidence supports these motive claims. He ran Detroit’s health department and has publicly argued leaders must address “unpaid debts” to Black communities; the Congressional Black Caucus PAC has endorsed him. [5][8][9]
  5. Opinion
    “Rogers completely obliterated El-Sayed; if it were a fight they would have stopped it.”
    Who won is subjective. Coverage described a hostile, mutual exchange in which both interrupted, traded personal attacks, and kept arguing into a break—not a one-sided rout. [1][2]
  6. Opinion
    “El-Sayed’s statements were loaded with lies and deceit, and he is short on patriotism and truth.”
    A blanket character attack that identifies no particular statement. Insults such as “lunatic” and “short on stature” are rhetoric, not checkable facts.
  7. Misleading
    “Trump won Michigan BIG.”
    Trump won Michigan in 2024, about 49.7% to 48.3% (roughly 80,000 votes, about 1.4 points). That is a real but narrow battleground win, not a large one. He lost the state in 2020. [10][11]
  8. True
    “Trump supports Mike Rogers for Senate and presents him as the candidate for Michigan voters.”
    Trump has endorsed Rogers, and this post restates that support. [12]
