Claims checked

  1. True
    “About 125 million people voted in the first round of Brazil's presidential election on October 4, 2026.”
    Brazil held its first-round presidential election on October 4, 2026. Official figures reported about 125.3 million voters, close to the post's 125 million. [1][2]
  2. Mostly true
    “Brazil's results came out shortly after polls closed, on the same day.”
    Brazil's electronic system began releasing results as soon as polls closed at 5 p.m. local time. By late election night, essentially all ballot boxes had been counted (about 99.9–99.99%), with a runoff set for October 25. A fully finished official count spilled slightly past midnight, so 'shortly after' and 'same day' are slightly loose but directionally accurate. [3][4]
  3. False
    “In Detroit, Philadelphia, California and other U.S. jurisdictions, results with much smaller numbers often take weeks to calculate because the process is used to rig elections.”
    Some large jurisdictions, especially California, can take days or weeks to finish counting mail and provisional ballots and to certify results. That delay comes from legal verification and canvassing rules, not from a scheme to rig the outcome. Fact-checkers have rated the claim that post-Election Day counting is how elections are cheated as false. [5][6][7]
  4. Opinion
    “Voting in America is corrupt.”
    A sweeping accusation of systemic corruption, not a specific checkable fact. The implied factual basis — that slow counts equal rigging — is false, but the slogan itself is rhetoric.
