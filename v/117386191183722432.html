Claims checked

  1. False
    “125 million people voted in the first round of India’s presidential election.”
    India does not elect its president by popular vote. An electoral college of elected MPs and state legislators chooses the president; Droupadi Murmu has held the office since 2022, and no presidential poll was held. Brazil’s first-round presidential election was on Oct. 4, 2026, and the electoral court reported about 125.2 million people voted. [1][2][3]
  2. False
    “Results from that large presidential vote came out shortly after polls closed, on the same day.”
    There was no Indian presidential vote whose results could have been released. Brazil did publish a near-complete first-round count the same night on its electronic system, with a runoff set because no candidate reached 50%. [3][4]
  3. Misleading
    “In Detroit, Philadelphia, California, and other U.S. cities and states, results with much smaller numbers often take weeks to calculate.”
    California can take weeks: mail ballots may arrive for seven days and counties have about 30 days to finish the official canvass. Detroit and Philadelphia do not typically take weeks. In 2024, Michigan reached about 95% of the presidential vote in roughly 9 hours and Pennsylvania in about 8 hours. [5][6][7]
  4. Opinion
    “Those delays exist in order to rig elections, and voting in America is corrupt.”
    The “Rig, I mean, calculate” line and the blanket charge that voting is corrupt are rhetorical accusations. The post supplies no evidence of fraud in the places it names.
