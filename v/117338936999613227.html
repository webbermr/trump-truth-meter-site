MisleadingAccuracy 50/100

The main news is true: on Sept. 26, 2026 Trump said he had approved new, much weaker fuel economy (CAFE) standards, and the final rule was expected Monday. But calling the Biden rules an "EV mandate," saying no chargers were built, and saying families will save "thousands" all stretch or leave out key facts.

Claims checked

  1. True
    “Trump approved new Fuel Economy Standards rolling back Biden-era rules”
    CNBC, Al Jazeera and Reuters (via the Spokesman-Review) reported on Sept. 26 that Trump approved sharply lower CAFE standards. NHTSA was expected to finalize them Monday.
  2. Misleading
    “The Biden standards were an 'EV Mandate' that forced Americans into cars they never wanted”
    The Biden rules raised fleet-wide fuel economy targets to about 50 mpg by 2031, which pushed automakers toward EVs. But they did not require anyone to buy an EV, and gas cars were still sold.
  3. Misleading
    “Billions were wasted on chargers that were never built”
    The $7.5B federal charger program was slow to roll out, and much of the money had not yet paid for working chargers. But some chargers were built, and most of the money had not been spent, so it was not simply wasted.
  4. Misleading
    “New standards will save families thousands on a new car”
    The administration's own estimate in its earlier proposal was an average saving of roughly $1,000 per vehicle, not several thousand. Buyers would also pay more for fuel over time.
  5. Mostly true
    “Over $100 billion is being invested in American autos under his administration”
    GM, Stellantis, Hyundai and other automakers have announced large U.S. investments that could add up to roughly this much. The total depends on what gets counted, and some of it is plans rather than money already spent.
  6. Unverifiable
    “Every manufacturer has called me wanting to build here”
    His private calls cannot be checked, and the claim is likely exaggerated.
  7. Opinion
    “AMERICA IS BACK; environmental monsters; plants coming back”
    These are slogans and descriptions, not facts that can be checked.
