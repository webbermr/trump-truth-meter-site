Claims checked

  1. True
    “The Fed inspector general’s report found “deficiencies in the management of the renovation project,” including that the Board did not get a project cost estimate from the construction manager until January 2026—3.5 years after construction began and after more than $2 billion in awards—and as of July 2026 still had not set a guaranteed maximum price, so it lacked cost certainty and had not transferred material risk.”
    The OIG report and same-day coverage use that language. The Board obtained the construction manager’s first project-wide estimate in January 2026 and had not agreed a guaranteed maximum price as of July 2026, after more than $2 billion had been awarded. [1][2][3]
  2. Misleading
    “The complex is hundreds of millions over budget and, according to the report, will cost at least $2.5 billion, with no end in sight.”
    Costs rose sharply: an early estimate near $1.9 billion later became a board-approved budget of about $2.38 billion, so “hundreds of millions over” earlier figures is fair and “at least $2.5 billion according to the report” is not. The report does not set a $2.5 billion floor and notes a projected construction end around December 2027, while warning cost certainty is still missing. [1][2][4]
  3. Unverifiable
    “The building is over budget “at a Record Setting rate.””
    The overrun is large, but neither the report nor coverage establishes that the rate of overrun is a record. [1]
  4. False
    “Powell is still managing the Fed’s high-interest-rate policy.”
    Powell remains a governor, but Kevin Warsh became Federal Reserve and FOMC chair on May 22, 2026. The FOMC’s latest rate decision, on September 16, 2026, was issued under Warsh. [5][6][7]
  5. Misleading
    “The report supports forcing Powell out or suing him for corruption or incompetence.”
    The IG found management failures and made recommendations, but reported no reasonable grounds for a criminal referral and no administrative misconduct. Day-to-day management was delegated to staff. Whether he should resign is an opinion, not a finding of corruption. [2][3][8]
  6. False
    “Trump asked Attorney General Todd Blanche to study the report.”
    Todd Blanche is Deputy Attorney General. The attorney general is Pam Bondi. A private request to Blanche could not be independently confirmed. [9][10]
  7. Opinion
    “Soundproofing and finishes will be inferior, historic sculptured ceilings and walls have been destroyed, and the final cost will be $3.5–4 billion; Trump could have renovated it for $25 million and does not want the building named after him.”
    The higher totals and the $25 million boast are speculation. Aesthetic judgments are not findings of the cost-control report. Plans described by the Fed include restoration of historic rooms and stonework, which undercuts a flat claim that the building’s historic character was simply destroyed, but quality of future finishes is not settled by the IG review. The naming denial is a personal statement. [1][4]
