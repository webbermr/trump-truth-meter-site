Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “Trump stated numerous times that it would take 4–6 weeks to get rid of the Iran nuclear threat.”
    He and the White House repeatedly used a roughly 4–5 or 4–6 week estimate, but that timeline was for the broader military campaign (missiles, navy, proxies, and nuclear sites), not a standalone plan to eliminate only the nuclear threat. He more often said four weeks or four to five weeks than a fixed 4–6. [1][2][3]
  2. False
    “He got rid of the Iran nuclear threat in one night.”
    U.S. forces struck Fordow, Natanz, and Isfahan overnight on June 21–22, 2025, and later fighting damaged other sites, but the nuclear threat was not eliminated. The IAEA’s September 2026 safeguards report says it still cannot verify the status or location of Iran’s enriched uranium or rule out diversion. Specialists describe a badly damaged program with a residual, non-imminent danger, not a threat that was erased in one night. [4][5][6]
  3. Misleading
    “The rest of the time since that night has only been to make sure the nuclear threat stays gone.”
    The administration’s stated objectives included destroying missile and drone forces, the navy, and the defense industrial base, not only locking in a completed nuclear success. The war that began in late February 2026 was still unresolved months later, and Trump himself has said it continued because he wanted to go further so Iran would not return in another form. [1][7][8]
