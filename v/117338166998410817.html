Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “The story that Donalds took Trump off his website and campaign materials is fake, with no sources”
    The Miami Herald, CNN and CBS documented at least eight Trump references removed from the homepage and issues page, including a 'Trump Endorsed' banner and photos. The sites themselves were the evidence. Trump was not removed entirely, and his endorsement still appears in a quote carousel and on the endorsements page.
  2. False
    “The story started with USA Today”
    CBS and others say the Miami Herald first reported the changes on Tuesday, Sept. 22.
  3. False
    “I won Florida by Millions of Votes”
    Official Florida results show margins of about 113,000 votes in 2016, 372,000 in 2020 and 1.43 million in 2024. None of these is 'millions.'
  4. Mostly true
    “Highest vote tally in Florida history, three times”
    His Florida totals rose each time: about 4.62M in 2016, 5.67M in 2020 and 6.11M in 2024. Each was a record for a candidate in the state at that time.
  5. Unverifiable
    “More popular in Florida now than at the last election”
    He cites no data for this. Coverage of the story describes Republican candidates putting distance between themselves and an unpopular president.
  6. Misleading
    “Byron immediately put out this picture and said the story was not true”
    Donalds posted the Oval Office photo and said he was proud of the endorsement. He did not deny that the website had changed, and he steered attention to his policy page.
  7. Unverifiable
    “Two rallies are already scheduled in Florida for Byron and will be sold out”
    Trump announced two rallies, but no dates or venues had been confirmed. 'Sold out' is a prediction.
  8. Mostly true
    “Donalds is leading David Jolly”
    A Stetson poll has Donalds ahead 51-39, but St. Pete Polls shows a near tie at 44-43.
  9. False
    “2020 was a totally rigged election”
    No court, the Justice Department or election-security officials found fraud that changed the 2020 result.
