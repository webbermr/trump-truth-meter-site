The 2025 Korea investment pact is real, and a roughly $22 billion Encinal, Texas, gas plant has been selected. The post treats a $200 billion ceiling, a non-binding eight-reactor framework, and a still-conditional Alaska LNG role as launched deals that are already turning into construction and jobs.

Editor’s note: All three reviewers agreed the 2025 $350 billion pact and the Encinal plant are real, and that the “trillions have come into” line is misleading. Claude treated the nuclear and Alaska announcements as mostly accurate “up to” frameworks and scored the post mostly truthful; ChatGPT and the earlier Grok review treated the same gaps — non-binding nuclear contracts, Seoul’s commercial-viability caveat on Alaska LNG, and the $200 billion figure being last year’s pledge rather than a new New York deal — as the central framing. I sided with that reading because contemporaneous Korean and wire reporting shows only the Texas project as selected, so presenting the package as launched construction and jobs is misleading rather than a minor hedge.

Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “In New York, Trump met President Lee Jae Myung and they agreed to launch $200 billion of new investments in America.”
    They met in New York on Sept. 22, 2026, and discussed the investment talks. The $200 billion is the strategic-industry portion of a 2025 pledge, not a new sum agreed at that meeting or on Sept. 30. Trump unveiled three candidate projects on Sept. 30; Seoul says only the Texas plant is finalized, and annual cash outlays under the pact are capped at $20 billion. [1][2][3][4]
  2. True
    “The projects fall under a $350 billion Korea trade and investment deal reached last year.”
    In 2025 South Korea pledged $350 billion in U.S. investment — about $200 billion in capped strategic investments plus $150 billion for shipbuilding — in exchange for most U.S. tariffs on Korean goods falling from 25% to 15%. A joint fact sheet followed in November 2025. Calling him “President Myung” swaps his family name (Lee) with his given name. [3][5]
  3. Misleading
    “The U.S. and Korea are moving forward on a $120 billion program to build eight large U.S. reactors: six Westinghouse AP1000s and two Korean APR-1400s.”
    Officials and Westinghouse describe a non-binding framework for up to about $120 billion and that reactor mix (up to two APR-1400s). Korean officials say commercial-reasonableness review still applies, contracts are not signed, and sites and regulatory approval are not settled. That is not eight reactors under construction. [2][6][7][8]
  4. Misleading
    “The U.S. and Korea have agreed to start work together on a $50 billion Alaska LNG project.”
    Trump announced Korean participation and U.S. officials cited about $54 billion, not a settled $50 billion stake. Seoul says size and terms still need negotiation and that it will not proceed unless the project is commercially and legally viable. The developer has not made a final investment decision. [2][6][9][10]
  5. Mostly true
    “There is a roughly $22 billion, 6.4-gigawatt natural-gas power project in Encinal, Texas.”
    This is the one project Seoul has formally selected. Developers announced about $22.3 billion and roughly 6.5 GW (about 6,470 MW), so the post’s figures are close roundings. Capacity and jobs are still prospective and depend on permits; commercial operation has been targeted around 2029. [2][6][11]
  6. Misleading
    “These commitments are being turned into construction and tens of thousands of American jobs.”
    No official combined jobs total was found. Texas developers have projected on the order of 8,400 peak construction jobs, a forecast rather than jobs already created, and the nuclear and Alaska projects are not committed builds. Presenting the package as already producing tens of thousands of jobs overstates where the deals stand. [2][11]
    🔁 Checked once before.
  7. Misleading
    “These projects support trillions of dollars of investment that have come into the United States under this administration.”
    The White House tally of “trillions” counts announced pledges, multi-year goals, and purchase commitments, not money that has already entered the country. Actual new foreign direct investment is far smaller. This repeats an earlier misleading claim. [12][13]
