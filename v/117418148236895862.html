  1. False
    “Democrats and the media are creating a false narrative that Trump is so unpopular Republicans will lose.”
    Reporting that Trump is unpopular tracks major surveys, not a fabricated narrative. Pew's Sept. 28–Oct. 4 survey found 30% approval and 68% disapproval, and an 8-point Democratic lead in House vote preference. Polls do not decide the election, but they support the premise he calls false. [1][2][3]
  2. False
    “Trump is so popular that the opposite is true.”
    The same surveys show net disapproval of roughly 16 to 38 points. Calling that popularity is not supported. [1][2][3]
  3. False
    “Current rallies are record-setting and prove that popularity and a Republican win.”
    No attendance figures in the post, and no independent evidence that these midterm rallies set records. The Clarksville venue, F&M Bank Arena, holds about 6,000; local reporting described a packed house with overflow turned away, which is a full small arena, not a record relative to his past stadium crowds. Reporting on other 2026 stops (Alabama, Ohio) described smaller crowds and empty sections compared with earlier rallies. Rally size also cannot prove a future election result. [4][5][6][7]

    A GOP midterm rally with Trump was scheduled for Oct. 10, 2026, at F&M Bank Arena in Clarksville, with remarks around 2:30 p.m. Central—shortly after this 18:59 GMT post. Local coverage confirmed the event that afternoon. [4][8]
  5. Opinion
    “Republicans are going to win.”
    This is a prediction about the Nov. 3, 2026 midterms, not a checkable fact.
