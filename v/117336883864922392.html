Calling that figure savings for *ratepayers* wrongly suggests it is an estimate of electricity-bill savings; EPA also projects some lower future electricity rates, but does not put their savings at $310 billion. Claims checked

  1. True
    “The Trump EPA rolled back Biden-era power-plant carbon rules.”
    EPA announced its final partial repeal on September 14, 2026; it proposed, but had not finalized, repeal of the remaining standards.
  2. Misleading
    “The rollback could save ratepayers $310 billion.”
    The $310 billion is EPA’s modeled present value of economy-wide social-cost savings for 2026–2047, not ratepayers’ electricity-bill savings. EPA separately estimates $160 billion in power-sector compliance savings at a 3% discount rate and projects lower average retail electricity rates over much of the modeled period.
  3. True
    “U.S. crude oil and dry natural gas production went on to set records.”
    The link preview’s production claim is supported by EIA figures showing record annual output for both in 2025.
  4. Opinion
    “Biden-era climate rules threatened coal plants, new gas generation and export projects.”
    The preview attributes this assessment to Republican officials and fossil-fuel advocates rather than establishing it as a measured outcome.
