Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “The Trump administration killed Biden’s fuel economy rules.”
    On Sept. 28, 2026, DOT and NHTSA finalized a reset of CAFE standards to about 34.5–34.9 mpg fleetwide by model year 2031, down from about 50.4 mpg under the Biden-era rule, and published it in the Federal Register on Sept. 30. The standards were weakened, not abolished; weaker rules remain and still require gradual efficiency gains. [1][2][3]
    🔁 Checked 2 times before.
  2. Misleading
    “New cars will cost $1,300 less under the new standards.”
    DOT said the rule would lower the average cost of a new vehicle by about $1,300. NHTSA’s own estimate is about $1,289 less in upfront regulatory costs for a model-year 2031 vehicle, and only if manufacturers pass those savings to buyers. The same analysis projects roughly $1,600–$1,700 more in lifetime fuel spending, which can cancel the sticker savings, and analysts say buyers should not expect a visible $1,300 price cut on cars sold now. [1][2][4][5]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Sep 26).
  3. Misleading
    “The new standards end what the administration calls Biden’s illegal EV mandate.”
    The link card correctly attributes the ‘illegal EV mandate’ label to the administration. The Biden CAFE rules were strict fleet-average targets that, with EV credits, pushed automakers toward more electric vehicles; they did not require Americans to buy EVs, and calling them illegal is the administration’s reading of the law, not a court finding. [1][2][6]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Sep 26).
