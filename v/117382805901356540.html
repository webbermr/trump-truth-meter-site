Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “Trump held packed election rallies in Ohio the night of Oct. 3 and in Alabama, Texas, and Oklahoma over the previous two days.”
    He rallied in Oklahoma City on Oct. 1, Mobile, Alabama, on Oct. 2, and Vandalia, Ohio, on the evening of Oct. 3. The Oct. 1 Denton, Texas, stop was a speech at a Peterbilt truck plant, not a fourth arena rally, and 'past two days' compresses a three-day swing. [1][2][3][4]
  2. Misleading
    “The rallies were all sold out, with thousands of people trying to get in.”
    Ohio was described as a capacity crowd of about 8,000 to 10,000 at a former airport hangar, with some reports of people still outside. Alabama's Mobile Civic Center rally was large, but photos and reporting showed visible empty seats, not a sold-out arena with thousands turned away. No independent count confirms thousands turned away at every stop. [3][5][6][7]
  3. Misleading
    “Fox & Friends Sunday was not covering those rallies, and nothing about them showed up on Fox, while other networks reported them very positively.”
    Fox News published multiple stories on the Ohio rally on Oct. 3 and again early Oct. 4, before this post. Whether that morning's Fox & Friends Sunday broadcast led with the rallies could not be confirmed from a rundown. Other coverage existed but was not uniformly glowing; CNBC, for example, led with a 'crude' Ohio speech. [5][8][9][10]
  4. Misleading
    “Fox spends more time talking to Communists, Socialists, and Democrats such as Jessica Tarlov, often without rebuttal.”
    Jessica Tarlov is a rotating co-host of Fox's The Five and regularly debates conservative panelists, so the 'without rebuttal' framing is wrong. Calling those guests communists or socialists, and claiming they get more airtime than Trump coverage, is unsupported rhetoric. A prior check found Fox does book Democrats including Tarlov. [11][12]

  5. False
    “Fox fired Maria Bartiromo because she complained about not being allowed to cover Trump.”
    Fox News Media said on Sept. 3, 2026, that it had parted ways with Bartiromo. Her lawyer denied she was fired. Reporting tied the split to her sharing internal editorial guidance with a Trump associate, not to a complaint that she was barred from covering Trump rallies. Grok's claim that she still hosts and was never separated is contradicted by Fox's own announcement. [13][14][15]
  6. False
    “Fox previously tried withholding this kind of coverage, almost went out of business, and did so right before an election.”
    Fox News has never nearly gone out of business. After the 2020 election it lost some viewers to Newsmax and others, then remained the top-rated cable news network. Third-quarter 2026 Nielsen figures still had Fox well ahead of CNN and MS NOW. [16][17]
  7. Opinion
