Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “The administration will immediately begin sending checks of nearly $100 to over 20 million seniors for Medicare Part B premiums, paid from the Medicare Improvement Fund.”
    Hours after the post there is no White House or CMS notice that such checks are going out. By statute the fund is for original-Medicare payment adjustments, not beneficiary premium rebates, and CBO has said no money has ever been spent from it. Its balance is only roughly in the neighborhood of $100 times 20 million. [3][4][5]
  2. False
    “Medicare Part B premiums have already been reduced by significant amounts.”
    CMS set the 2026 standard Part B premium at $202.90, up $17.90 (about 10%) from $185 in 2025. Officials said action on skin-substitute spending avoided a larger increase of roughly $11 a month; that slowed a rise, it did not cut the premium. [1]
  3. False
    “The Medicare Improvement Fund is a pointless slush fund used only by Democrats in Congress for waste, fraud, abuse, and special interests that drive up healthcare costs.”
    Congress created the fund in 2008 as a budget-scoring placeholder. CBO and other analysts say it has been amended many times and that no money has ever been spent from it on program operations by either party. [3][4]
  4. Misleading
    “Most Favored Nation drug deals, together with this fund, are substantially lowering costs for seniors.”
    The administration has announced MFN pricing agreements with many drugmakers, largely for Medicaid and selected products. That does not establish a broad cut in seniors’ Part B costs: the standard premium is rising nearly 10% in 2026. [1][6]
  5. Misleading
    “Medicare and Social Security were on the verge of ruin under Joe Biden.”
    Trustees have long projected depletion in the early 2030s (OASI around 2033; Medicare’s hospital-insurance fund also around 2033 in recent reports). Those shortfalls predate Biden and were not an imminent collapse during his term. [7][8]
  6. False
    “Joe Biden let tens of millions of illegal aliens into the country.”
    Border encounters exceeded 10 million over FY2021–FY2024, but encounters are not successful entries, and millions were expelled or removed. Independent estimates put the rise in the unauthorized population at roughly 3 to 4 million, and Pew put the total unauthorized population at about 14 million in 2023—not tens of millions admitted under Biden. [9][10]
  7. Misleading
    “The administration is sending substantial refund checks to one million Americans who were overcharged for Affordable Care Act plans by Joe Biden.”
    The White House announced about $500 payments to nearly one million unsubsidized HealthCare.gov enrollees, and reporting says payments have begun, so “one million” is roughly right. Experts dispute that Biden uniquely overcharged them: a large user-fee surplus already existed before he took office, and KFF has said his administration lowered marketplace user fees. [2][11][12]
  8. Misleading
    “A highly popular $5,000 Trump Dividend will be distributed to every U.S. citizen if Republicans win the midterms.”
    This is a conditional campaign promise, not an authorized payment. Trump previously described $5,000 for adult citizens, not every citizen including children. Analysts put the adult-only cost above $1 trillion, and Congress would have to appropriate it. Republicans already control both chambers. [13][14]
