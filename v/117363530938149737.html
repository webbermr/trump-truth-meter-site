Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “Trump ousted CNN from White House access.”
    On Sept. 18, 2026, Trump announced he was banning CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House, and their credentials were pulled. A federal judge ordered access restored days later, so it was a real but short-lived ban, not a lasting ouster. [1][2]
  2. Misleading
    “Jack Smith spied on news outlets.”
    Grassley disclosed that messages involving nearly 50 journalists were in records Smith obtained. Those messages came from subpoenaed White House officials’ phones, not from subpoenas or surveillance directed at the news outlets. Critics call it a First Amendment problem; the word “spying” implies the journalists were targeted, which the disclosures do not establish. [3][4][5]
  3. True
    “The records came from National Archives productions tied to 87 White House phones and captured journalist-source exchanges.”
    A June 2023 special-counsel subpoena to the National Archives sought texts associated with 87 Trump White House phones. The production included officials’ exchanges with journalists. [3][4][6]
  4. Mostly true
    “Smith’s team obtained those records while pursuing Trump over both the 2020 election and classified documents.”
    The phone production covered roughly October 2020 through Jan. 20, 2021 and was part of the election/Jan. 6 investigation (Arctic Frost). Tying the same phone records equally to the separate classified-documents case blends two investigations. [4][5][6]
  5. Opinion
    “Media coverage of the CNN ban and the Smith disclosure exposes a double standard.”
    Whether the difference in coverage is a double standard is an editorial judgment, not a checkable fact.
