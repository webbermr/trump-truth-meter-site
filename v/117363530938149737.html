Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “Trump ousted CNN from White House access.”
    On Sept. 18, 2026, Trump announced he was barring CNN, MS NOW, and Politico from the White House; reporters were denied entry. A federal judge ordered access restored and the outlets were reinstated around Sept. 24, so “ouster” overstates a brief, court-reversed ban. [1][5][6]
  2. Misleading
    “Jack Smith spied on news outlets.”
    Grassley disclosed that Smith’s team obtained National Archives text records from about 87 White House phones that included exchanges between officials and nearly 50 journalists. Those journalists were not investigation targets; this was collection of officials’ archived government-phone records, not wiretaps or direct surveillance of news organizations. [2][3][4][7]
  3. Opinion
    “Media coverage of the CNN ban versus the Smith records exposes a double standard.”
    Whether the difference in emphasis is a double standard is a judgment, not a checkable fact.
  4. True
    “The records came from National Archives productions tied to 87 White House phones and captured journalist–source exchanges.”
    Senate Judiciary, Fox News, and the Washington Examiner report that a June 2023 subpoena to the National Archives for records associated with 87 White House phones produced texts that included communications between government sources and nearly 50 journalists. [2][3][4]
  5. Mostly true
    “Those records were gathered as Smith’s team pursued Trump over the 2020 election and classified documents.”
    Smith investigated both matters. The specific White House phone-text subpoena covered October 2020 through January 20, 2021, and was part of the post-election probe, not a search of classified-document files. Linking these particular texts to the documents case is not established. [4][8][9]
