Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “The Kennedy Center is crumbling and a great danger to anyone who walks inside.”
    On Sept. 4, 2026, a roughly 4-by-5-foot section of Grand Foyer plaster fell about 60 feet during a storm and scattered debris on the carpet, matching the photo. No one was injured. The center later cited corrosion and safety risks and closed the main building, but an annex stayed open and the record does not show that every person who walks inside faces great danger. [1][2][3]
  2. Misleading
    “The building has been in this dangerous condition for years and will only get worse unless repairs proceed.”
    The 1971 building has long had deferred maintenance and water intrusion, and the center itself blamed “decades of neglect.” Engineers found corrosion in overhead panels. Whistle-blowers said known leak repairs were shelved and then recast as an acute emergency. “Will only get worse” is a prediction. [1][4][5]
  3. Misleading
    “A federal judge is the obstacle preventing necessary repairs.”
    U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper blocked putting Trump’s name on the building without congressional approval and required process or notice before a multi-year closure or demolition. His orders do not ban ordinary structural repairs. Trump and the Justice Department have tied the renovation to reversing the naming ruling. [6][7][8]
  4. True
    “The judge has a lawyer wife.”
    Cooper is married to attorney Amy Jeffress. Calling her “Trump Hating,” and calling him a “Radical Left Judge,” are characterizations, not established facts. [9]
