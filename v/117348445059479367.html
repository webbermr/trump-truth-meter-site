Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “The Supreme Court unleashed voter citizenship checks.”
    On Sept. 25, 2026, the Court stayed a lower-court injunction and allowed the administration to resume using the expanded SAVE database so states can check voters' citizenship. It was an emergency stay while the lawsuit continues, not a final ruling on the merits. [1][2][3]
  2. Misleading
    “Although split along party affiliation, the Supreme Court underscored the validity of Trump's plans to shore up election integrity.”
    The three liberal justices dissented from an unsigned stay, an ideological split that tracks the party of the appointing president, but justices have no formal party affiliation. The order concerned the expanded SAVE system and did not finally uphold Trump's broader election-integrity plans. [1][2][3]
  3. Misleading
    “CISA put voting machines on a leash by issuing a report telling election offices to treat infrastructure as a year-round target.”
    CISA did release its 2026 Election Infrastructure Security Plan on Sept. 24 and said securing election infrastructure requires year-round diligence. Recommendations such as paper ballots and audits are voluntary; CISA says state and local officials have primary responsibility and its services are provided only on request. That is not a federal restraint on voting machines, and the plan does not tell offices to treat infrastructure as a target. [4][5][6]
  4. Opinion
    “Trump stacks wins.”
    Triumphal framing of the two developments, not a checkable factual claim.
