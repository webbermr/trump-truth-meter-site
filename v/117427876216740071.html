Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “Roy Cooper let out 83 murderers, including 51 people serving life sentences.”
    A February 2021 court settlement of an NAACP/ACLU lawsuit led to early reentry or community supervision for thousands of inmates. Reporting and a partisan database put roughly 50–70 people with murder convictions, and about 51 life-sentence inmates, on related lists—not a documented 83 murderers Cooper personally released. Officials said the lifers were already parole-eligible, and the parole commission, not Cooper, decided individual releases. Some finished sentences in 2021 anyway. [1][2][3][4]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Oct 11).
  2. False
    “25 of those released immediately committed murders after being let out.”
    Even the higher campaign-style counts are later rearrests or charges over years, not 25 immediate murders by people Cooper released. The New York Post reported 18 later murder charges among a broad COVID-era release group. The Assembly found several of the cited cases involved people already free before the settlement, and only one killing clearly fell in a window created by the early release. Ads linking unrelated murders to the settlement have been rated false or misleading. [2][5][6][7]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 11).
  3. Misleading
    “Cooper released them because he did not want them to catch COVID in prison.”
    COVID deaths and conditions were why civil-rights groups sued. Cooper’s administration had resisted broader releases and commutation requests; it settled after a judge signaled the prisons were likely unconstitutional, to avoid a larger court-ordered release. Presenting it as his personal wish to spare inmates COVID omits that legal pressure. [1][3][8]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Oct 11).
  4. Opinion
    “There has never been a governor with such a bad record on crime, and there never will be.”
    Superlative political attack, not a checkable comparison of governors’ crime records.
  5. False
    “Dukakis lost an insurmountable 1988 lead because he released Willie Horton, who then immediately raped and killed three women.”
    Horton was on a weekend furlough under a program created before Dukakis and that Dukakis defended; Dukakis did not personally release or pardon him. While out, Horton raped one woman and assaulted and stabbed her fiancé, who survived—he did not kill three women. Dukakis’s post-convention lead was large but not insurmountable, and the race turned for several reasons, not solely Horton. [9][10]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 11).
  6. Opinion
    “What Cooper did is far more consequential than the Horton case.”
    Comparative political judgment, not a factual claim.
  7. True
    “Trump is endorsing Michael Whatley for U.S. Senate from North Carolina.”
    Whatley is the Republican nominee for the North Carolina Senate seat, and Trump has endorsed him. [11]
    🔁 Checked 3 times before.
