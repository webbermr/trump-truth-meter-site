Claims checked

  1. False
    “The New York Times reported on the Ohio rally that “the place was not full. Some upper sections were almost completely empty.””
    That wording tracks the Times’ October 2 account of the Mobile, Alabama rally, not a verbatim quote about Vandalia, Ohio. The Times and its pool reporter did separately describe empty or emptying upper seating at the October 3 Ohio stop, so the underlying complaint that the paper described empty seats is not invented—but the attributed quotation is. [1][2][3]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 5).
  2. False
    “The Ohio venue was totally maxed out, including all upper sections, with no empty seats.”
    It was Vandalia-Butler High School’s student activity center, capacity roughly 4,500–5,000, not a large arena. NBC and the White House pool reported stretches of empty upper bleachers when Trump took the stage and more emptying as the speech went on. Close video of the floor does not show every section. [2][3][4]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 5).
  3. Misleading
    “Thousands of people were unable to get in and had to be turned away.”
    Reporters saw a long line and parking limits before doors opened, and Trump himself said people were outside. That does not establish thousands turned away: the gym holds about 5,000, and empty upper seats were visible once he spoke. In the attached clip he even asks people to give up seats. [3][4][5]
    🔁 Rated misleading 2 times before, since Oct 4.
  4. Misleading
    “Empty upper seats are people migrating down to the main floor, which always happens and which the Times knows.”
    Pool notes described people streaming out of an upper deck and side seats about 45 minutes into the speech—leaving, not filling the floor. Some movement between sections is possible, but it does not explain the documented empty sections. [2][3]
  5. False
    “Trump never has empty seats, has not for years, and these events are more crowded than ever.”
    Empty sections were reported at this Ohio stop, at the Mobile rally the day before, and at other recent appearances, including the Dallas convention. The Times described these midterm stops as more modest than his old standing-room-only rallies. [1][3][6]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 5).
  6. False
    “Networks refuse under any circumstances to show the crowds.”
    NBC, local stations, and photographers published coverage and images from inside and outside the Ohio event. An absolute blackout claim is not supportable. [3][7]
  7. Misleading
    “When Bernie Sanders draws 500 to 1,000 the media treats it as the biggest event ever, while a Trump crowd of 50,000 is described as if nobody showed up.”
    The Ohio gym holds about 5,000, not 50,000, and major outlets covered the rally in detail, including crowd and empty-seat observations. Sanders events have also drawn far more than 500–1,000 (a 2025 Los Angeles rally with AOC was reported around 36,000). The double-standard comparison is unsupported exaggeration. [3][8]
    🔁 Rated misleading 2 times before, since Oct 4.
  8. Opinion
    “The New York Times and other outlets should be arrested for dishonesty.”
    A demand for punishment, not a checkable fact.
  9. True
    “He was heading to a rally in Nebraska that night.”
    The post went up Monday afternoon UTC. He was scheduled to rally that evening in Grand Island, Nebraska, for Sen. Pete Ricketts. [9][10]
