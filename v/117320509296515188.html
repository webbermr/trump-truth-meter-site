Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “Canada is allowing millions and millions of people into the country.”
    From 2022–2024 Canada admitted record permanent residents and a temporary-resident population that peaked near 3 million. By mid-2026 that surge had reversed: Q2 2026 saw about 99,000 new permanent immigrants, and the non-permanent resident population fell by roughly 155,000 over the year to July 1, 2026, with population declines in recent quarters.
  2. False
    “Entrants are essentially unchecked and unvetted.”
    IRCC security screening checks permanent and temporary applicants against Canadian and partner databases, with extra review for higher-risk cases. Gaps and backlogs have been documented, but that is not the same as essentially no vetting.
  3. Misleading
    “Problems are already showing up as big unemployment caused by those arrivals.”
    Statistics Canada’s August 2026 Labour Force Survey put unemployment at 6.4%, unchanged from July after earlier declines, with a net job loss that month. That rate is elevated versus the tight post-pandemic market, but it is not extreme by historical standards, and the figures do not show that immigration is the cause—especially while inflows and the temporary population were shrinking.
  4. Opinion
    “This is a Liberal takeover that will destroy Canada and end very badly.”
    Political characterization and prediction, not a checkable fact. The Liberals have governed federally since 2015 and set the immigration targets.
