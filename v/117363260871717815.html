Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “Smith’s investigative team accessed White House text messages without first following the designated filter-team privilege review.”
    A Justice Department letter to Grassley says the special counsel’s investigative team “apparently bypassed the Filter Team and directly accessed” text messages obtained from the National Archives. Records indicate senior lawyer Thomas Windom downloaded the files within about half an hour of receipt on Aug. 21, 2023, and others began reviewing them within an hour. DOJ’s “apparently” is a hedge, but the timing is documented. [2][3][6]
  2. Misleading
    “Jack Smith’s team ignored attorney-client privilege.”
    The filter team existed to screen attorney-client and work-product material, so bypassing it was a serious protocol lapse and created a risk to privileged material. Calling that an established act of ignoring attorney-client privilege overstates the record: DOJ did not find that the messages accessed were attorney-client privileged, and no court has ruled that a privilege was violated. [1][2][3]
  3. Misleading
    “Jack Smith’s team ignored Speech or Debate privileges of members of Congress.”
    The filter protocol did not screen for Speech or Debate material, the team had been warned of constitutional risk, and investigators reviewed texts between White House officials and 44 lawmakers of both parties. Whether those texts were protected legislative acts, and whether the clause was violated, is contested and unadjudicated. Smith testified that his office took the clause seriously and consulted DOJ’s Public Integrity Section. The New York Times notes no court has held the clause bars this kind of records collection. [2][3][4][5]
  4. Opinion
    “Smith’s team did this “to get Trump.””
    The investigation did target Trump, but “to get Trump” is a motive slogan, not a checkable fact.
