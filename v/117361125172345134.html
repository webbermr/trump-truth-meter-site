Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “The last American forces and equipment left Iraq on 30 September 2026 in an orderly departure from Erbil Air Base.”
    CENTCOM said the coalition completed an orderly departure of forces and equipment from Erbil on 30 September 2026, ending the military mission in Iraq. A bilateral security relationship and some US personnel in non-coalition roles can remain, so 'the last American Forces' slightly overstates a total US exit. [1][2]
  2. Mostly true
    “Ali al-Zaidi is Iraq's new prime minister, whom Trump supported and fully endorsed from the beginning.”
    Parliament confirmed Ali al-Zaidi (also spelled Al-Zaydi) as prime minister in May 2026. Trump publicly backed him during government formation and opposed other candidates; 'from the beginning' and 'fully endorsed' is his characterization, not a documented early formal endorsement. [3][4][5]
  3. False
    “Al-Zaidi won an election he was not expected to win, in a landslide.”
    Iraqis elected a parliament in November 2025; al-Zaidi was not on a national ballot as a candidate for prime minister and did not win a personal popular-vote landslide. He was nominated and confirmed by parliament after coalition bargaining. [3][4][6]
  4. False
    “Operation Inherent Resolve was launched in 2003 under George W. Bush and continued under Obama in 2014 and under Biden.”
    The 2003 invasion was Operation Iraqi Freedom. Operation Inherent Resolve, the campaign against ISIS, was established in 2014 under President Obama. Bush was not president then and did not launch it. [7][8]
  5. Misleading
    “Operation Inherent Resolve ends in 2026 under President Trump with the Iraq departure.”
    The mission in Iraq ended on 30 September 2026 under Trump. CENTCOM said the task force remains operational, headquartered in Jordan, with a continuing counter-ISIS mission in Syria. The post also omits the US–Iraq timetable announced in September 2024, before his second term. [1][9]
  6. Misleading
    “ISIS has been completely decimated by Trump's first term, and there is no longer a caliphate.”
    ISIS lost its last territorial caliphate in 2019, during Trump's first term, after a campaign begun under Obama with partner forces. It was not completely destroyed: US assessments in 2026 still treated ISIS branches and remnants as active threats. [7][10]
  7. Mostly true
    “Unlike Afghanistan, where military equipment was left behind and 13 US service members were killed, this Iraq departure did not repeat that outcome.”
    Thirteen US service members were killed at Abbey Gate in August 2021, and large amounts of US-funded equipment remained in Afghanistan, much of it previously transferred to Afghan forces. Reporting on the 2026 Erbil exit describes a planned handover without a comparable abandonment or US combat deaths during the departure. [1][11]
