U.S. and coalition forces did complete an orderly departure from Erbil on September 30, 2026, ending the Operation Inherent Resolve mission in Iraq. The post still misstates how Ali al-Zaidi became prime minister, treats the 2003 invasion as the start of OIR, omits the 2024 U.S.–Iraq withdrawal timetable, and overstates a total defeat of ISIS.

Editor’s note: All three reviewers agreed the Erbil departure and the end of the Iraq OIR mission are real, and that the post is misleading overall. They split on Ali al-Zaidi: Grok treated the name as invented and said Sudani was still prime minister, which later reporting contradicts; ChatGPT and Claude correctly have al-Zaidi as PM but not as a landslide election winner. I followed the better-sourced view that he is PM via parliamentary selection, kept the 2003/OIR conflation as false, and weighted the omitted 2024 Biden-era timetable and the overstated ISIS victory as the main misleading frames.

Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “The last American and coalition forces and equipment are leaving Iraq from Erbil Air Base, ending the U.S. military mission there.”
    CENTCOM and multiple outlets reported that the last coalition troops completed an orderly departure from Erbil Air Base on September 30, 2026, and that the Operation Inherent Resolve mission in Iraq had ended. A U.S. diplomatic and security-cooperation presence can remain without combat troops, so “the last American Forces” is slightly broader than the military mission that actually closed. [1][2][3]
  2. Mostly true
    “Iraq’s prime minister is Ali al-Zaidi, whom Trump supported and endorsed.”
    Reporting confirms Ali al-Zaidi was serving as Iraq’s prime minister by the time of the withdrawal, after parliament approved his government in May 2026. Trump publicly associated himself with al-Zaidi; early, decisive endorsement “from the beginning” is not clearly documented and is softer than the post implies. [2][4][5]
  3. False
    “Al-Zaidi won an election he was not expected to win, in a landslide.”
    Iraq’s prime minister is chosen by parliament, not by a direct popular vote. The November 2025 parliamentary election produced no landslide; Sudani’s bloc led with a modest share, and al-Zaidi, described as an independent, was nominated and confirmed by parliament rather than elected in a surprise landslide. [4][5][6]
  4. False
    “Operation Inherent Resolve was launched in 2003 under George W. Bush and continued under Obama in 2014.”
    The 2003 invasion was Operation Iraqi Freedom, a separate war. CENTCOM designated anti-ISIS operations as Operation Inherent Resolve in October 2014, during the Obama administration. [7][8]
  5. Misleading
    “Operation Inherent Resolve ends in 2026 under President Trump, who chose to end a presence that would otherwise have become permanent.”
    The Iraq mission of OIR did conclude on September 30, 2026, while Trump was president. The United States and Iraq had already agreed in September 2024, under Biden, to a two-phase transition ending coalition operations in Iraq by September 2026. CENTCOM also said counter-ISIS work continues from outside Iraq, including Syria. Trump oversaw the final step; he did not originate the timetable. [1][3][9]
  6. Misleading
    “ISIS has been completely decimated by the Trump administration in the first term, and there is no longer a caliphate.”
    The territorial caliphate ended in March 2019, during Trump’s first term, after a multinational campaign that began in 2014. ISIS was not completely eliminated: cells and a residual threat have persisted, and U.S. forces continue counter-ISIS operations outside Iraq. [1][10][11]
  7. Mostly true
    “Unlike Afghanistan, where much military equipment was left behind and 13 U.S. service members were killed, this Iraq departure was orderly.”
    Thirteen U.S. service members were killed and dozens wounded in the August 2021 Abbey Gate attack, and large quantities of U.S.-funded equipment transferred to Afghan forces were left behind in the chaotic withdrawal. Contemporary accounts describe the 2026 Erbil exit as planned and orderly, with coalition equipment departing with the troops. The Afghanistan comparison slightly blurs equipment given to Afghan forces with gear simply abandoned by U.S. units. [1][2][12][13]
  8. Opinion
    “This is a great day, al-Zaidi is outstanding, and it was time for America to go home.”
    Praise, judgment, and the decision framing are opinion, not checkable facts.
