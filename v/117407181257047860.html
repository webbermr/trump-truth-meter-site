Claims checked

  1. False
    “Minnesota is being taken over by Somali immigrants.”
    Minnesota has the largest U.S. Somali community, on the order of 85,000–110,000 people of Somali ancestry, including many born in the United States — roughly 1.5–2% of a state of about 5.8 million, concentrated in the Twin Cities. That is not a takeover of the state. [1][2]
  2. Misleading
    “There is violence, murder, and mayhem all over Minnesota, associated with Somalis.”
    In early October 2026, police and local officials described a string of Twin Cities-suburb shootings, including a large gun battle, as linked to Somali American gangs, and some mayors called the situation serious. That is localized, not statewide mayhem. Minnesota’s 2025 Uniform Crime Report showed violent crime down about 13% and homicides down sharply, and the FBI Minneapolis office said violent crime was still falling in 2026. [3][4][5][6]
  3. Misleading
    “Minnesotans have been robbed and abandoned by their state government.”
    Federal prosecutors have charged dozens of people, many of Somali descent, in Feeding Our Future and related schemes involving hundreds of millions in stolen public funds, and oversight failures have been widely criticized. The state has also announced anti-fraud measures and its Bureau of Criminal Apprehension is coordinating a response to the recent gang shootings, so “abandoned” overstates the record. [3][7][8]
  4. False
    “Governor Tim Walz is an incompetent thief who wants to leave with as much as he can steal and lead a quiet life.”
    Fraud cases have charged private defendants and nonprofits, not Walz personally, and no credible source shows he is stealing public money or planning to abscond with it. The “get out of town” motive is an unsupported accusation, not a documented fact. [1][7][8]
  5. False
    “Walz does not want to get involved.”
    The state Bureau of Criminal Apprehension is meeting with local agencies about the gang shootings, and Walz’s office has put forward an anti-fraud package. That contradicts a categorical claim that he will not get involved. [3][8]
  6. Misleading
    “Governor Tim Walz and Minneapolis Mayor Jacob Frey must immediately ask for federal help, and are not doing so.”
    Reporting does not show Walz or Frey requesting the kind of federal intervention Trump is offering, which matches the earlier finding that they were not asking for his help. Their request is not a legal prerequisite for federal law enforcement: the FBI can investigate federal crimes without a governor’s invitation and already works with Minnesota agencies. [9][10][11]
