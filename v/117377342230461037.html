Claims checked

  1. False
    “Trump won Alaska every single time he ran.”
    He carried Alaska in the 2016, 2020, and 2024 general elections, but Ted Cruz beat him in the 2016 Alaska Republican caucus, about 36% to 34%. [1][2]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Oct 3).
  2. Opinion
    “Trump brought more back to Alaska than anybody in human history.”
    A superlative with no measure or comparison that can be checked.
  3. True
    “Dan Sullivan is a Marine and the incumbent U.S. senator from Alaska.”
    Daniel Scott Sullivan has been Alaska’s senator since 2015 and retired in 2024 as a colonel in the Marine Corps Reserve. [3][4]
  4. Mostly true
    “Sullivan was a pivotal vote for rebuilding the military and for opening ANWR.”
    He voted for the 2017 tax law that opened ANWR’s coastal plain to leasing and has been a leading advocate for Alaska defense and energy. Calling one senator the pivotal vote is rhetorical; the tax bill passed 51-48, so every Republican vote was needed. [5]
  5. False
    “As a House member, Mary Peltola voted for every policy of the Biden administration.”
    She voted with Biden on a large share of contested 2023 votes, about 80% in one tracker, but she broke with him often, including by pushing the Willow oil project and voting against her party on border and other bills. [6][7][8]
  6. Misleading
    “Peltola voted for a catastrophic energy policy that would destroy Alaska and caused lots of inflation.”
    She voted against the Republican Lower Energy Costs Act, but she publicly championed Willow and opposed some Biden restrictions on Alaska development. No identified vote of hers is shown to have caused inflation. [7][6]
  7. Misleading
    “Peltola voted for wide-open borders.”
    She voted no on the 2023 Secure the Border Act, but she later backed border-enforcement bills, called for stronger border security, and voted to condemn the Biden administration’s handling of the border. [8][9]
  8. Misleading
    “Peltola voted for men playing in women’s sports.”
    In 2023 she voted against the Protection of Women and Girls in Sports Act and called it bullying and federal overreach. That is a vote against a ban, recast as a vote for men in women’s sports. [10]
  9. Mostly true
    “Trump won Alaska by a lot.”
    He won Alaska by about 15 points in 2016, 10 points in 2020, and 13 points in 2024 (54.5% to 41.4%). Those are solid wins, not landslides. [2][11]
  10. Misleading
    “Democrats recruited a spoiler also named Dan Sullivan, who knew nothing about politics, solely to confuse voters and cheat.”
    Daniel J. Sullivan Jr., a retired Petersburg teacher and first-time candidate, is on the November ballot. He registered as a Republican the day he filed, first asked to be listed as Dan S. Sullivan, and a Democratic consultant helped him file. The state elections director found he appeared to be trying to confuse voters. Democrats, the Peltola campaign, and the candidate denied recruitment, and reporters found no proof of a party plot. Calling it proven Democratic cheating goes beyond the evidence. [4][12][13][14]
  11. True
    “The election is November 3 and the incumbent to vote for is Dan S. Sullivan.”
    Alaska’s 2026 general election is November 3. The incumbent is Daniel Scott Sullivan, listed to distinguish him from Daniel J. Sullivan Jr. [4][15]
