Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “A government watchdog concluded that the Biden administration risked security by letting millions of foreigners overstay visas, including threats.”
    DHS OIG report OIG-26-38 (Sept. 29, 2026) found ICE received 3,145,375 nonimmigrant overstay leads in FY2021–FY2024, categorized 104,049 as potential national-security or public-safety concerns, and said people who potentially posed threats remained at large after limited lead-driven action (496 investigations and eight lead-driven arrests). Those figures are leads and potential concerns, not millions of confirmed overstays or verified threats; only 21,885 of the 104,049 were deemed viable. The report does not conclude that Biden personally “let millions overstay,” and it also cites unverifiable addresses and insufficient resources. FY2021 partly predates the Biden administration. [1][2][3]
  2. Mostly true
    “The report exposes a Biden strategy that de-emphasized tracking down visa overstays and left a large enforcement gap.”
    The OIG traced limited lead-driven action in part to a September 2021 policy directing Homeland Security Investigations to pursue only “Priority 1” leads. Of 1,769 viable Priority 1 leads, HSI opened 84 investigations and made two arrests; most other leads were Non-Priority. That is a real deprioritization. The report also blames resource limits and bad address data, and ERO made 16,458 overstay arrests outside this lead process. “Staggering statistical mess” is rhetorical. [1][2]
