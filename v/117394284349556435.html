Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “Fox News spends more time covering Democratic (“Jihadist and Dumocrat”) candidates than Republicans and is promoting Democrats rather than Republicans and MAGA.”
    No airtime audit supports the comparison. Fox’s recent output still centers Republican and Trump stories, including an interview with El-Sayed’s Republican opponent Mike Rogers and next-day clips of Trump’s Ohio and Nebraska rallies. One afternoon interview does not show that Democratic candidates are “all they talk about.” [1][2][3]
  2. False
    “Abdul El-Sayed is a jihadist.”
    El-Sayed is the Democratic nominee for U.S. Senate in Michigan, a physician and former Detroit health director. No evidence in the interview or his public record supports calling him a jihadist; that is an unsupported smear. [4][5]
  3. Misleading
    “Martha MacCallum gave El-Sayed a softball interview.”
    She interviewed him on The Story on October 5, 2026. Fox’s own clip and reports across the spectrum say she pressed him on Soros-linked donors, terrorism comments, the Muslim Brotherhood, and Iran; outlets called the exchange fiery or combative, not soft. [6][7][8]
  4. Misleading
    “MacCallum never interviews Trump the way she interviewed El-Sayed.”
    Whether an interview feels equally friendly is subjective, but MacCallum has interviewed Trump, including a September 18, 2025 sit-down in which she questioned him on designating antifa a terrorist group. The “never” claim is not accurate. [9]
  5. Misleading
    “Fox does not show Trump’s rallies the morning after, while other outlets do, and those rallies are record-breaking.”
    This repeats an earlier, already-misleading complaint about Fox ignoring rallies. Fox streamed the October 3 Ohio and October 5 Nebraska rallies and published next-day clips and reports. Recent events were in modest venues—an Ohio high-school gym of about 4,500 and a Nebraska hall at its roughly 5,800 capacity—not documented record crowds. [2][3][10][11]
