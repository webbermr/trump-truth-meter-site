Claims checked

  1. False
    “Trump won Alaska every time, in the primaries and general elections — about six times.”
    He carried Alaska in the 2016, 2020, and 2024 general elections, and won the 2024 Alaska Republican primary. He lost the March 2016 Alaska GOP caucus to Ted Cruz (about 36% to 34%), and Alaska Republicans canceled the 2020 presidential primary. That is not six wins. [1][2][3]
  2. True
    “Nick Begich is Alaska’s U.S. representative and the election to reelect him is November 3.”
    Begich holds Alaska’s at-large House seat and is seeking reelection. Alaska’s 2026 general election is November 3. [4][5]
  3. Misleading
    “Begich was a pivotal vote for the largest tax cuts in American history, including no tax on tips, no tax on overtime, and no tax on Social Security.”
    He voted for the 2025 reconciliation law, which passed the House narrowly and created capped, temporary deductions for qualified tips and overtime plus an extra deduction for many seniors. It did not repeal federal taxation of Social Security benefits. Relative to the economy, the package is not clearly the largest tax cut in U.S. history; much of its cost is extending the 2017 cuts. His yes vote was one of a narrow House majority, not a uniquely decisive swing. [6][7][8][9]
  4. True
    “Every Democrat voted against those tax cuts.”
    No Democrat voted for final passage of the One Big Beautiful Bill in the House or the Senate. The vote was on the whole bill, not a separate vote on each tax provision. [6][10]
  5. Mostly true
    “Begich helped win record funding for border security.”
    The same 2025 law included the largest single package of immigration-enforcement and border funding ever enacted, on the order of $150–170 billion, and Begich voted for it. He was one of 218 House yes votes, not the author of the funding. [6][11]
  6. Misleading
    “Trump secured the Alaska LNG pipeline, the largest energy project in Alaska history, after decades of attempts.”
    A North Slope gas export line has been pursued for decades and has recent federal prioritization, permits, and developer activity. As of early October 2026 it was still in preliminary planning, without a final investment decision or signed long-term supply and export contracts. Announced foreign funding has also been disputed. It is not a completed or assured project. [12][13]
  7. Mostly true
    “With Begich’s support, Alaska LNG will create over 13,000 jobs and save households up to $1,400 a year.”
    Developer materials project roughly 12,000 construction jobs plus about 1,000 long-term operating jobs, and residential energy savings on the order of $1,400–$1,450 a year. Those are forecasts for an unbuilt project, not jobs or savings already delivered, and construction jobs would not all be simultaneous or all Alaskan. [12][14]
    🔁 Checked once before.
  8. Misleading
    “Bill Hill is a radical-left socialist who supported AOC and other Democrat communists.”
    Hill, a Bristol Bay fisherman and former educator, is running as an independent and is the main challenger, with support from the Alaska Democratic Party and the DCCC. Public records show a small donation to Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez. That does not make him a socialist or a communist, and he is not a Democratic nominee. [15][16][17]
  9. Misleading
    “Hill would vote to raise taxes by $5 trillion.”
    The figure tracks the roughly $4–5 trillion, 10-year cost Republicans assign to letting the 2017 tax cuts expire, not a Hill proposal. His platform calls for higher taxes on billionaires and corporations and relief for small business and the middle class, with no $5 trillion tax increase. [17][18]
    🔁 Rated misleading once before (Oct 3).
  10. Unverifiable
    “Trump has brought more federal money back to Alaska than any other president.”
    No defined comparison of federal spending or projects in Alaska across presidencies supports the superlative. It is campaign boasting.
