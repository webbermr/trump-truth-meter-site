Claims checked

  1. True
    “Promotional ads featuring Trump have been paid for with U.S. government money.”
    DHS committed about $20 million, via Customs and Border Protection, for a national media campaign. The spots end with “Paid for by the U.S. Government,” and trackers put airtime spending in the millions. [1][2][3]
  2. Misleading
    “Paying for these ads with government money is a rather standard thing to do.”
    Agencies have long run public-information and recruitment ads, which the White House cites. These spots instead promote Trump personally, and at least one closely matches a 2024 campaign ad. Experts and lawmakers say that is not a normal use of taxpayer funds. Calling it flatly illegal overstates the record: publicity-and-propaganda limits are real, but courts and agencies have not treated every presidential appearance in a government ad as a clear violation of 5 U.S.C. § 3107. [4][5][6]
  3. Misleading
    “The people upset about the taxpayer-funded ads are the Radical Left.”
    Democrats have objected, including Senate appropriators. Republicans have too, including Senate Majority Leader John Thune and Sen. John Kennedy, who said public money should not pay for the ads. [1][6][7]
  4. Unverifiable
    “He has decided to pay for the patriotic ads and others himself and with money raised for MAGA Inc.”
    This post announces a decision. Through early October, reporting still described the ads as taxpayer-funded, even after he said on Sept. 30 he would “gladly pay” if told the spending was wrong. MAGA Inc. has large cash reserves, but there is no independent confirmation that he or the PAC has replaced the government funding. [3][8][9]
  5. Opinion
    “The ads are a positive promotion for the U.S.A.”
    That is his characterization of the ads, not a checkable fact.
