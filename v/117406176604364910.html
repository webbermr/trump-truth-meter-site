Claims checked

  1. True
    “The White House has directed use of “Super Intelligence” instead of “Artificial Intelligence.””
    On September 29, 2026, Executive Order 14434 told executive agencies, to the extent permitted by law, to use “Super Intelligence”/“SI” rather than “Artificial Intelligence”/“AI” in official correspondence and communications, and not to acknowledge the older terms in those settings. The order covers agency usage, not the public at large. [1][2]
  2. False
    ““Super Intelligence” is the highly accepted new and more accurate term for artificial intelligence.”
    The rebrand is an administration choice, not a widely accepted replacement. In technical usage, “superintelligence” means a hypothetical AI that surpasses human intelligence across domains, which researchers generally say does not yet exist. The order itself still defines the new label as covering the existing statutory meaning of artificial intelligence, so it is not more accurate. [1][3][4]
  3. Misleading
    “The White House considers anyone who uses “Artificial Intelligence” instead of “Super Intelligence” to be the enemy.”
    Read as a policy claim, this is false: EO 14434 is a terminology directive for executive-branch communications and does not label journalists, companies, or private speakers as enemies, nor punish use of “AI” outside official settings. “THE ENEMY!” is also rhetorical hyperbole, but it is presented as a White House position resting on a real order, so the line is misleading rather than pure opinion. [1][2]
