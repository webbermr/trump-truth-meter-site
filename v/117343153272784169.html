Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “More people are working in the United States right now than at any point in the country’s history.”
    BLS’s August 2026 household survey, which counts employed people, showed 162.746 million employed, below December 2025’s 163.992 million and below August 2025’s 163.370 million. Seasonally adjusted nonfarm payrolls, a jobs count that can include the same person more than once, were 159.075 million after a 162,000 gain and at a series high. The employment-population ratio was 59.1 percent, down from 59.6 percent a year earlier and far below the April 2000 peak of 64.7 percent. Unemployment was 4.1 percent. September’s report was not out yet on September 27. PolitiFact rated a similar line Half True in March 2026; the missing context is what makes the boast misleading. [1][2][3][4][5][6]

Each AI reviewer’s verdict

    While the total number of employed Americans frequently sets raw record highs as the population grows, the claim is misleading because it ignores population growth and the employment-to-population ratio.
  • Claude Misleading 60

  • ChatGPT Misleading 40

Payroll jobs can count one person more than once, so the record does not establish the post’s claim.
  • Grok Misleading 40
