Trump did win Missouri by large margins in 2016, 2020, and 2024. His claim that the Supreme Court has three times overruled judges and denied Missouri an “Election Victory” misstates the map fight: the Court’s September 25, 2026 order kept the 2022 map in place for the still-unheld midterms, and not all three emergency actions overruled judges who had sided with the new map.

Editor’s note: All four reviewers agreed Trump wrote the post and that his three large Missouri presidential wins are accurate. They split on the Court claim: Gemini treated it as pure opinion and scored the post truthful, while Grok, Claude, and ChatGPT treated “three times… overrule Judges” as checkable and found it imprecise. Primary reporting and the September 25 order support the narrower reading—three emergency actions, but only the later two set aside orders favoring the 2025 map, and none decided a completed election—so the central framing is misleading rather than merely opinion or mostly accurate.

Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “The U.S. Supreme Court has three times overruled judges who favored Missouri’s new congressional map.”
    The Court acted three times on emergency applications about the 2025 map. On September 8, Justice Kavanaugh declined to block a Missouri Supreme Court ruling against the map, so he did not overrule a judge who had sided with it. On September 10 and September 25, the Court stayed or set aside federal orders that had favored using the 2025 map. The September 25 order was unanimous and concerned which map to use for an election that had not yet been held. [1][2][3]
  2. Misleading
    “The Supreme Court will not let Missouri have an election victory.”
    The orders address which congressional map applies to the November 2026 election, which had not concluded. They did not overturn a completed election result. Calling that a denial of an “Election Victory” frames a map dispute as a stolen election. [1][2]
  3. Opinion
    “Lower-court judges came to the correct decisions.”
    Whether those rulings were legally correct is a judgment, not a checkable fact.
  4. True
    “Trump won Missouri big in all three of his presidential elections.”
    Official results show he carried Missouri in 2016 by about 18.5 points, in 2020 by about 15.4 points, and in 2024 by about 18.4 points. [4][5][6]
