Claims checked

  1. Opinion
    “The nation is doing better than ever before in many ways.”
    This is a broad, subjective assessment. Recent data mix record household income with solid but not historic GDP growth, unemployment above the 2023 low, and weaker consumer confidence, so it is not a checkable fact that conditions are better than ever. [1][2][3]
  2. Unverifiable
    “The public just doesn’t know how well the nation is doing.”
    The post gives no evidence about public awareness, and disagreement with the administration’s assessment is not the same as not knowing the numbers.
  3. Misleading
    “The administration has record-setting numbers.”
    Some series are at records: the Census Bureau said real median household income in 2025 was the highest on record, and the S&P 500 has traded near highs. Other core measures are not: BEA’s second estimate put Q2 2026 real GDP growth at 2.2%, well below past peaks, and August unemployment was 4.3%, above the 3.4% low in 2023. The post treats unnamed “Record Setting Numbers” as proof of unprecedented national performance. [1][2][4][5]
    🔁 Rated false once before (Sep 27).
  4. False
    “The Fake News Media refuses to disseminate those record-setting numbers.”
    Major outlets are publishing the official figures. AP reported the Census income and poverty release (with context on Trump’s claims) and, on the day of this post, the BEA’s upward revision of Q2 growth to 2.2%. AP also covered August job openings the day before. Coverage often includes caveats, but that is not a refusal to report the numbers. [6][7][8]
    🔁 Rated false 3 times before, since Sep 26.
