Claims checked

  1. Opinion
    “Democrats ("Dumocrats") are scammers.”
    This is a political insult, not a checkable claim.
  2. Mostly true
    “Crude oil was above $100 on 308 days in 2009–2017.”
    A recount of EIA Cushing WTI daily spot prices puts Obama's two terms at about 297 trading days above $100, not 308—close, but high. The chart does not name the benchmark. On Brent, the same window is roughly 850-plus days, mostly from the 2011–2014 supply shock, not a presidential price setting. [1][2][3]
  3. True
    “Crude oil was above $100 on 0 days in 2017–2021.”
    Neither WTI nor Brent closed above $100 in Trump's first term. EIA annual WTI highs in those years stayed in roughly the $60–$77 range, and WTI briefly went negative in April 2020. [1][2][4]
  4. Mostly true
    “Crude oil was above $100 on 83 days in 2021–2025.”
    EIA WTI closes above $100 in Biden's term total about 87 days, almost all in the 2022 spike after Russia's invasion of Ukraine, not 83. Brent was higher, about 115 days. The count is close for WTI and low if "crude" means Brent. [1][2][5]
  5. False
    “Crude oil has been above $100 on 24 days from 2025 to Oct. 9, 2026.”
    The 24 figure matches an older mid-2026 cutoff that was already circulating on X. By early October, EIA WTI closes above $100 in the second term were about 31 (a March–May stretch plus mid-September), and Brent about 66 and still above $100. On Oct. 8, 2026 Brent settled near $104 while WTI was about $91, so "crude oil" above $100 was not a closed chapter of 24 days. A term under two years old is also not comparable to full four- and eight-year terms. [1][3][6][7][8]
