Claims checked

  1. Misleading
    “Roy Cooper let out 83 murderers, including 51 prisoners serving life sentences.”
    A February 2021 settlement of an NAACP lawsuit over COVID prison conditions put about 3,500 people into existing early-release channels. About 51 people serving life sentences appeared on the related list, but state officials said they were already parole-eligible, not lifers without parole freed by a Cooper order. Independent and partisan tallies put murder convictions on the broader list around 70, not 83. Cooper had refused COVID commutations; the deal followed litigation and a court finding that conditions were likely unconstitutional. [1][2][3]
  2. False
    “Those released prisoners committed 25 murders immediately after being released.”
    No official or independent count supports 25 murders committed immediately by people Cooper released. A widely cited figure is 18 later murder charges among the larger group of early releases; fact-checkers found that misleading because several people were already out, or would have been released before the alleged crime anyway. A partisan database counted far fewer reoffenders among people convicted of murder, not 25 new murders. [1][2][4]
  3. Misleading
    “Cooper released them because he did not want them to catch COVID in prison.”
    Reducing COVID risk was the purpose of the lawsuit and the February 25, 2021 settlement. Cooper’s administration had resisted broad releases, refused to commute sentences, and agreed to a narrower program after a judge’s ruling, implemented through corrections and parole officials rather than personal releases by the governor. [1][2][5]
  4. True
    “DeCarlos Brown Jr. fatally slashed Iryna Zarutska from behind while she sat on a train, and the attack was captured on security video.”
    On August 22, 2025, Brown fatally stabbed Zarutska from behind as she sat on a Charlotte light-rail train. Surveillance video captured the attack. He was charged with murder in state court and later in federal court. The charge is an allegation, not a conviction. [6][7]
  5. False
    “Brown was put on the streets through a release by Cooper.”
    State prison officials said Brown completed his mandatory minimum for armed robbery and was released on September 20, 2020, about five months before the COVID settlement. The settlement did not cause his release. His name later appeared on settlement-related records because of a post-release proceeding, not because Cooper freed him early. [1][6][7]
  6. False
    “Michael Dukakis released and pardoned Willie Horton, who then kidnapped, raped, and murdered again, costing Dukakis the 1988 election despite an insurmountable lead.”
    Horton was serving life for a 1974 murder and, while on a weekend furlough, committed armed robbery, kidnapping, and rape in 1986–87. He was not pardoned, and he did not commit another murder in that episode. The furlough program began under a prior Republican governor; Dukakis vetoed a bill that would have barred furloughs for first-degree lifers. Dukakis led by about 17 points in a July 1988 poll, a lead that had already narrowed before the Horton ads, and lost to Bush. The furlough issue hurt him, but it was not a pardon that alone caused the defeat. [8][9]
  7. True
    “Michael Whatley headed the North Carolina Republican Party and then the national Republican Party, and Trump is endorsing him for U.S. Senate.”
    Whatley chaired the North Carolina GOP from 2019 into 2024 and the Republican National Committee from March 2024 until he resigned in 2025 to run. He is the 2026 Republican nominee for North Carolina’s open Senate seat, and this post is Trump’s endorsement. Calling those chairmanships “unprecedented Victories,” and saying he will never let voters down, is opinion. [10]
