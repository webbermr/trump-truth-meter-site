The September flash PMI did show unusually strong private-sector activity, including a 62-month high in the composite, and 2025 real median household income was a Census record. The post misstates the services high, treats a one-month survey spike as proof of the world’s hottest economy and the fastest second-term growth since 2015, and leaves out the same report’s rising costs and weak goods exports.

Editor’s note: All three reviewers agreed the composite 62-month high, the August 2022 CPI figure, the 2025 median-income record, and the oil claim are solid, and that the voice is Trump-like but staff-structured. They split on overall verdict because Claude treated the services error as a harmless understatement and left the “fastest growth since 2015” line unchecked, while ChatGPT and Grok correctly tied that line to S&P’s survey wording and to weaker official GDP. I follow that reading: the services “52-month high” is simply wrong (it was 59), and presenting a one-month PMI spike, plus omitted cost and export weakness, as proof the second term is the world’s hottest economy is misleading rather than mostly accurate.

Claims checked

  1. True
    “The S&P flash composite index (services plus manufacturing) was at a 62-month high.”
    S&P Global’s 23 September 2026 flash release put the US Composite Output Index at 58.4, up from 56.0, and called it a 62-month high and the fastest expansion since July 2021. It is a business survey, not measured GDP. [1][2]
  2. Mostly true
    “Manufacturing was at a 53-month high.”
    Manufacturing output rose to 56.7, which S&P called a 53-month high. The headline Manufacturing PMI was 57.0, a 52-month high, so the post matches the output gauge rather than the main manufacturing PMI. [1][2]
  3. False
    “Services was at a 52-month high.”
    The flash Services Business Activity Index was 58.7, which S&P called a 59-month high. The 52-month figure belongs to the headline manufacturing PMI, not services. [1][2]
  4. Opinion
    “This is the hottest economy in the world.”
    Among major September flash composites the US reading was high, but “hottest economy in the world” is a superlative about the whole economy, not a checkable fact established by one PMI print. [1][3]
  5. Misleading
    “The economy is putting Americans back to work.”
    The PMI said firms added staff at the fastest pace since June 2022, and August payrolls rose 162,000 with unemployment at 4.1%. Labor-force participation was lower than a year earlier, so this is not a broad return-to-work surge. [1][4]
  6. True
    “Real median incomes are the highest on record.”
    Census reported real median household income of $87,460 in 2025, up 2.6% and the highest since the series began in 1967. That is an annual 2025 figure, not September 2026 incomes, and post-tax median income was not a record. [5][6]
  7. True
    “Biden inflation in August 2022 was over 8 percent.”
    BLS reported the CPI-U up 8.3% for the 12 months ending August 2022. [7]
  8. Misleading
    “Ignoring the post-COVID rebound, Trump’s second term would be the fastest growth since 2015.”
    S&P said this month’s improvement in business activity, barring the COVID reopening spike, was the greatest since early 2015. That is not second-term GDP growth. BEA put 2025 real GDP growth at 2.2%, and Q2 2026 real GDP grew at a 1.5% annualized rate. [1][8][9]
  9. Misleading
    “Deportations, tariffs, and federal workforce cuts did not hurt or slow the economy.”
    A strong flash survey does not show those policies had no drag. The same S&P release said goods export volumes continued to fall and that input-cost inflation was the fastest since October 2022. What growth would have been without the policies is not established. [1][2]
  10. Misleading
    “A hot economy is giving workers more wage leverage, and Americans are getting richer.”
    The 2025 median household-income gain supports part of this, but real average hourly earnings fell 0.3% over the year ending August 2026. The post presents a broad current gain for working people that these measures do not establish. [5][10]
  11. True
    “Oil did not go to $200 a barrel.”
    Oil has not reached $200. EIA’s September outlook put August Brent near $91 and forecast about $90 for the second half of 2026; WTI traded well below $200. [11]
  12. Opinion
    “The media is covering up success to help Democrats, and gas prices will come down.”
    The alleged motive is unsupported, and the gasoline-price statement is a prediction.
