Claims checked

  1. Mostly true
    “News coverage is saying Trump invited the enemy to bomb San Diego and Los Angeles.”
    Newsom’s office said he was advocating an attack and that foreign actors might read the remarks as an invitation. Mika Brzezinski said he was “casually calling for Iran to attack” the cities, and headlines cast an Iranian bombing as the “small price” he described. Not every outlet used the word “inviting.” [1][2][3]
  2. Misleading
    “What he actually said was only a comparison: a temporary, short rise in gasoline prices is a small price to pay so Iran does not get a nuclear weapon, versus the much larger cost if Iran bombed San Diego or Los Angeles.”
    On Oct. 5 he said gas prices were “a little bit high” and “a small price to pay,” then “Let them take out Los Angeles. Let them take out San Diego. That’s, this is a very small price to pay.” FactCheck.org finds a protective comparison more plausible than an invitation, and he used similar language before. He did not say “if you want to see a big price, imagine if they bombed” those cities; that clearer version came on Oct. 7, after the backlash, and he still said “let them hit” the cities. [1][2][4]
  3. False
    “Everybody, including the news media, knew he was only making that comparison.”
    The wording was widely called confusing. Democrats and some hosts treated it as a call to attack or as treating the cities’ destruction as acceptable. Asked to clarify on Oct. 6, Trump waved the question off. The White House later offered the comparison reading; that is not evidence the press already knew it and lied. [1][2][3]
  4. False
    “It is totally clear what he said.”
    FactCheck.org and contemporary coverage describe the Oct. 5 lines as confusing and open to the attack reading that produced the backlash. [1][2]
