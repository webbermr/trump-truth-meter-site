Claims checked

  1. False
    “Kaitlan Collins, CNN, and similar outlets said they were not going to cover Trump.”
    Reporting and Collins herself described the opposite: the White House restricted or barred CNN and other outlets from White House press access, and the television pool suspended its camera coverage because the White House blocked CNN from carrying out an assigned pool duty. That is not a statement by the outlets that they would stop covering him. Collins was credentialed by the United Nations and was present covering the trip. [1][2][3][4]
  2. True
    “Collins and other reporters were in the UN press area and confronting or questioning Trump.”
    Contemporaneous accounts say Trump confronted Collins in the UN press area and she asked him about the Iran war, telling him the United Nations had allowed the reporters in. [1][3][5]
  3. Misleading
    “They were ranting and raving like lunatics.”
    Accounts of the exchange describe questions and a short reply after Trump said she should not be there, not a rant. The wording greatly overstates what was reported. [1][3][5]
  4. Opinion
    “Collins has a “TRUMP” addiction; CNN and MSDNC are sick, treasonous, and demented; Collins is a third-rate, unhappy person.”
    These are insults and characterizations, not checkable facts.
  5. Opinion
    “The outlets have a judge who is great for them and totally disloyal.”
    Calling an unnamed judge “totally disloyal” is a subjective attack. The post does not identify a case or ruling. Related litigation over press access has involved Judge Timothy J. Kelly, a 2017 Trump nominee, but loyalty is not a factual finding. [6][7]
